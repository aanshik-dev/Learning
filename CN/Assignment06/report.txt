Simulation settings
-------------------
H = 10000 frame-times
Fixed random seed = 42
G = 0.1 to 3.0 in steps of 0.1
CSMA propagation delay ratios tested: a = 0.01, 0.05, 0.10

Measured peaks
--------------
Pure Aloha:       S = 0.186 at G = 0.4
Slotted Aloha:    S = 0.373 at G = 0.9
CSMA a = 0.01:    S = 0.542 at G = 1.10
CSMA a = 0.05:    S = 0.520 at G = 1.00
CSMA a = 0.10:    S = 0.499 at G = 1.10

Analytical results
------------------
Pure Aloha: S = G exp(-2G), peak = 1/(2e) = 0.1839 at G = 0.5
Slotted Aloha: S = G exp(-G), peak = 1/e = 0.3679 at G = 1.0

Part D - Written analysis
------------------------
1. Why does slotting double the Pure Aloha peak?
Pure Aloha has a vulnerable window of two frame-times. Slotted Aloha
allows transmissions only at slot boundaries, reducing the vulnerable
window to one frame-time. The analytical peak therefore changes from
1/(2e) to 1/e, which is exactly twice as high.

2. Why does sensing beat both Aloha methods?
Aloha stations transmit without first checking the channel. 1-persistent
CSMA senses the channel and defers arrivals while it is busy, greatly
reducing unnecessary collisions. Collisions still occur because sensing
is not instantaneous: stations starting within propagation delay a can
both believe the channel is idle.

3. Why do the Aloha curves collapse at high load while CSMA does not?
As G increases, Aloha has a rapidly increasing probability that another
attempt falls inside its vulnerable window, so collisions dominate and
useful throughput falls. CSMA defers many attempts instead of allowing
them to overlap, so its throughput remains much higher.

4. What quantity controls how close CSMA comes to full utilisation?
The propagation delay ratio a controls the collision interval. Increasing
a makes the channel take longer to reflect a new transmission to other
stations, increasing collision opportunities and lowering the peak:
a=0.01 -> S=0.5313
a=0.05 -> S=0.5160
a=0.10 -> S=0.4973

5. Vulnerable windows
Pure Aloha:       2 frame-times
Slotted Aloha:    1 frame-time
1-persistent CSMA: approximately one propagation-delay interval a

The shorter vulnerable window of slotted Aloha explains its higher peak
than Pure Aloha. CSMA has a much smaller collision interval when a is
small, so it achieves substantially higher throughput.
