OVERVIEW
In this assignment, We have implemented OLS  and Batch-Mode Gradient Descent algorithms to predict Salary from Years of Experience using the Salary_Data.csv dataset 


DATASET ANALYSIS
- Dataset: 6699 samples,
- feature: Years of Experience, target: Salary.
- Mean salary: 115,327 , Std: 52,786
- Mean experience: 8.09 years , Std: 6.06
- The data shows a roughly linear positive trend between experience and salary.

GRADIENT DESCENT: HYPERPARAMETER TUNING
We used the best OLS split (4:6) to tune the learning rate with zero initialisation over 10,000 epochs.

  Learning Rate   Final w_0       Final w_1       R2        Mean-RSS
  0.0001          18179.45        10138.72        0.4585    1.51e+09
  0.001           57658.67        7047.41         0.6607    9.48e+08
  0.01            59426.82        6908.96         0.6602    9.49e+08

Observations:
- alpha = 0.0001: Too small. After 10,000 epochs the parameters have not converged (w0 = 18179, far from OLS value of 59427). R2 = 0.458 - under-converged.
- alpha = 0.001: OPTIMAL. Converges to w0 = 57659, w1 = 7047, R2 = 0.6607. Very close to the OLS solution, confirming convergence to the global minimum.
- alpha = 0.01: Also converges, but the slightly lower R2 compared to alpha=0.001.
A large step size causes the update to overshoot the minimum and explode to infinity.
Selected best learning rate: alpha = 0.001

Observations:
  - OLS and GD produce nearly identical results across all splits.
  - The small differences (< 0.002 in R2) are due to GD not fully converging to the
    exact closed-form solution within 10,000 epochs at alpha=0.001.
  - OLS is always exact (closed-form), while GD is approximate but scalable to larger
    datasets where matrix inversion (OLS) becomes expensive.
  - For this dataset and scale, OLS is preferred due to its exactness and speed.


8. CONCLUSION

  - Both OLS and Gradient Descent successfully fit a linear hypothesis for Salary prediction.
  - The best split was 4:6 (40:60), giving R2 ≈ 0.66 for both methods.
  - OLS converges to the exact solution instantly; GD closely approximates it with
    a well-tuned learning rate (alpha = 0.001) over 10,000 epochs.
  - R2 ≈ 0.66 suggests Years of Experience alone explains about 66% of salary variance.
